Cheque Bounce

Fast, effective NI Act Section 138 proceedings with a strong recovery record.

A dishonoured cheque is not merely a payment default — under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, it constitutes a criminal offence punishable with imprisonment of up to two years and a fine that may extend to twice the cheque amount. The law is designed to protect the integrity of cheque-based transactions, and strict compliance with its procedural requirements is what determines whether a complaint succeeds or fails.

The procedural steps under Section 138 are time-sensitive and must be executed precisely. A valid legal notice must be sent within 30 days of the cheque being returned by the bank, giving the drawer a 15-day window to make payment. If payment is not made within that period, a complaint must be filed with the Magistrate within 30 days of the expiry of the notice period. Missing any of these deadlines can result in the complaint being declared barred by limitation. We manage this entire process with meticulous attention to these strict timelines.

We represent both complainants — individuals and companies seeking recovery — and accused parties who are defending complaints. For complainants, our focus is on securing swift recovery, whether through the court process or through negotiated settlement during the pendency of proceedings. For the accused, we examine the complaint documents carefully for procedural defects and substantive defences, including lack of legally enforceable debt, payment already made, and discrepancies in signature or amount.

Compounding of the offence — settlement between the parties — is a frequently preferred outcome in cheque bounce matters, as it results in recovery for the complainant and discharge for the accused without the time and cost of a full trial. We negotiate effectively on behalf of our clients and ensure that any settlement terms are legally documented and enforceable. Where settlement is not possible, we are fully prepared to take matters through complete trial and, if required, appeal.
